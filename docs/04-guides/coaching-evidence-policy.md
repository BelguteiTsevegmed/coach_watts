# Coaching evidence and product policy register

Version: **coaching-evidence-v1**. Reviewed: **2026-10-08** (applies to every row). This is a focused claim review, not a systematic review of all coaching advice or a medical diagnosis feature. Evidence-strength labels below describe the reviewed study design and applicability; they are not a formal GRADE assessment unless explicitly stated.

Evidence describes a studied outcome in a particular population. Product policy decides what this application recommends or permits. A configurable limit, a display band or a prompt default is not evidence of injury prevention, overtraining diagnosis or medical/race clearance. Symptoms, explicit clinician restrictions and red flags take precedence.

## Versioning and remaining uncertainty

Bump `COACHING_EVIDENCE_VERSION` in `shared/coaching-evidence.ts` and update this register when changing reviewed claims or their policy interpretation. All central coaching prompts include version/review date; generation tracking retains full prompts. New structured generation/adjustment snapshots also store `coachingEvidenceVersion`, and new weekly plan JSON records it explicitly. Existing generations remain historical and are not relabelled. No database migration is needed.

The registry names current policy locations and distinguishes developer-configurable/injectable rules from advisory prompt defaults. It does not introduce medical clearance, a user override of contraindications, or an injury-risk score. Numeric caps in `plans/training/workout-safety-layer-plan.md` remain draft policy, with an explicit evidence review before issue #10 implements them.

Uncertainties remain around individual injury prediction, optimal recovery cadence and long-session shares, transport of research across sports/populations, missing training history, sensor quality and causal effectiveness of durability interventions. This change qualifies the central guidance and affected daily decisions; it does not establish every existing performance tooltip as an evidence-backed clinical rule.
